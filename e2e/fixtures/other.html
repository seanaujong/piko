Harbour Depths

A harbour is only as useful as its shallowest point at low water, which is why charted depths are quoted against a datum rather than against whatever the sea happens to be doing on the day of the survey.

Dredging buys depth at considerable expense and holds it only until the next season of silt arrives. Every port authority therefore keeps a running argument with the river that feeds it, and the river generally wins in the end.

The oldest harbours were chosen rather than built, which is a different kind of engineering and arguably the better kind. Somebody stood on a headland and noticed that the water stayed deep close in.
